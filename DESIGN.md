# Design decisions

The user supplied a second-round research summary: six genuine participants (a student commuter, a resident walking home at night, two local residents, a parent of primary-school children and a night-time driver). Four reviewed the concept; the other two contributed encounter and information-needs evidence. Four coding-practice transcripts are explicitly excluded from research claims. Counts below are reported from that summary, not independently recoded transcripts.

All six cared about wildlife but lacked confidence about intervention; five of six encountered wildlife while travelling or walking and could not research in the moment. All four concept reviewers expected to read more than post and preferred a browser website to another app. Five of five asked about review wanted human moderation; five of six supported broad public locations. Participants trusted professional sources over neighbour discussion and preferred stories and practical advice over statistics and games. Optional community activities needed clear organisers, duration, accessibility and safety expectations. The user also reports comparison with literature on displays, citizen science, moderation, privacy and QR interaction; no bibliographic details were provided, so none are invented here.

These findings support four primary areas: Local Story, Is It Hurt?, Meet the Animal, and Read, Share or Volunteer. A permanent safety link opens practical text and an attributed RSPCA Queensland contact without a QR scan. Neighbour reports and professional information are visually separated. Live publishing, an identifiable moderation organisation, text-only/optional-photo submission and “Sent for review” remain intended Fencepost requirements rather than implemented backend capabilities.

- **Story first:** one large animal photograph and one short observation replace the four-panel dashboard. Three sample stories rotate every 20 seconds, with next and pause controls. Reduced-motion preferences pause rotation initially. Observation text, approximate area and time, possible species, and a separately attributed wildlife fact distinguish neighbour reports from reference information. Photographs are illustrative. Stories are adapted from the existing prototype posts, without usernames, exact times, routes or addresses.
- **Colour:** warm cream (#f5f1e7) reduces the density of the former dark dashboard; forest green (#193c31) carries headings, text and navigation; pale sage (#e3e9da) groups learning content; rust (#944124) highlights content labels. This is a design response to the requested calm, legible park experience, not a claim that interviewees selected these colours. Text contrast exceeds WCAG AA for normal text against its intended backgrounds.
- **Less to choose:** home offers species learning and community participation, with a permanent injury-help link. Separate stories, quizzes, sounds, language controls, charts, statistics and map panels are removed from primary navigation. Legacy activity URLs redirect to their replacement. The two species pages each have one optional discovery, rather than an additional main feature: a safe-encounter question or a behaviour fact reveal.
- **Short visits:** large headings, short observation copy, high contrast and persistent safety guidance support glance reading. Most controls are at least 56 CSS pixels high; optional audio is 48 pixels. Navigation remains near the viewport bottom. Physical reach still needs testing on the installed display with children and seated visitors. After two minutes without pointer, touch, keyboard or scroll activity, secondary pages return home. Home resets to its first story. All content remains readable without audio; optional browser speech synthesis reads the current observation and fact.
- **Secondary community action:** a single habitat action card contains the prototype status and participation limits. It is explicitly a sample until an organiser, full date and verified registration URL are supplied. The existing Fencepost QR is used only for sharing, never passed off as an event registration code. Completed activities can later supply Community Update stories after editorial checks.

## Still needed for deployment

Retain the six-participant research basis and exclude coding-practice transcripts. Provide verified volunteer details and a registration destination before replacing the sample status with a registration QR. Connect a moderated, privacy-reviewed post source before claiming stories automatically derive from live community posts. Review specimen identification and fact sources with the content owner. Check screen height, reach, outdoor glare and audio on the physical kiosk.

## Source checking limitation

The cloud network denied requests to RSPCA Queensland and Queensland Government websites during this change (proxy 403). The linked RSPCA guidance/contact and Australian Museum species references must be checked with the content owner before public deployment; live source verification is not claimed. No AI diagnosis or species-specific rescue/handling procedure is provided.
